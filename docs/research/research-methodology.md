\# Research Methodology



\## Research Title



\*\*सुलभ प्रवास सहाय्य योजना: ग्रामीण महाविद्यालयीन विद्यार्थिनींसाठी डिजिटल प्रवास सहाय्य प्रणाली\*\*



\*\*Sulabh Pravas Sahayya Yojana: A Digital Travel Assistance System for Rural College-Going Female Students\*\*



\---



\## 1. Nature of the Research



This project follows an \*\*Applied Research\*\* approach because it attempts to understand practical travel-related problems experienced by rural college-going female students and develop a technology-based intervention to support their daily college travel.



\---



\## 2. Research Approach



A \*\*Mixed-Method Approach\*\* is adopted.



\### Quantitative Component



Quantitative information includes:



\- bus waiting time;

\- missed buses;

\- college attendance;

\- late arrivals;

\- transport-related absences;

\- frequency of travel problems;

\- Likert-scale responses;

\- usability and perceived-usefulness responses.



\### Qualitative Component



Qualitative information is collected through open-ended questions relating to:



\- major travel problems;

\- difficulties with bus services;

\- useful application features;

\- problems experienced while using the application;

\- suggested improvements;

\- additional features required; and

\- overall student feedback.



\---



\## 3. Research Design



The research is organised into multiple stages.



\### Stage 1 – Problem Identification



Travel-related problems of rural college-going female students are identified.



\### Stage 2 – Pre-Use Survey



A descriptive survey is conducted before introducing the digital intervention.



The survey focuses on:



\- bus availability;

\- bus timing;

\- waiting time;

\- missed buses;

\- route information;

\- bus-stop accessibility;

\- safety concerns;

\- late arrival;

\- transport-related absence; and

\- other travel difficulties.



\### Stage 3 – Digital Intervention



The \*\*Sulabh Pravas Sahayya Yojana\*\* mobile application is introduced as a digital travel-assistance intervention.



\### Stage 4 – Beta Testing



Participants use the application and evaluate its functionality and usability.



\### Stage 5 – During-Use Feedback



Feedback is collected regarding:



\- ease of use;

\- useful features;

\- technical difficulties;

\- travel assistance;

\- journey planning;

\- safety functions; and

\- suggestions for improvement.



\### Stage 6 – Post-Use Evaluation



Post-use feedback is collected to study:



\- perceived usefulness;

\- satisfaction;

\- usefulness of individual features;

\- perceived travel support; and

\- recommendations for further development.



\### Stage 7 – Long-Term PRE–POST Evaluation



A longer-term evaluation is proposed using a \*\*One-Group Pre-Test–Post-Test Pre-Experimental Design\*\*.



Potential PRE and POST indicators include:



\- attendance percentage;

\- late arrivals; and

\- transport-related absences.



\---



\## 4. Study Population



The target population consists of \*\*rural college-going female students who depend on bus/public transportation for travelling to college\*\*.



\---



\## 5. Pilot/Beta Sample



The initial pilot/beta study is designed for approximately \*\*30 participants\*\*.



Stable participant codes such as:



`SP001`, `SP002`, `SP003` ... `SP030`



may be used in research records to reduce the use of personally identifying information.



\---



\## 6. Sampling Method



\*\*Purposive Sampling\*\* is appropriate for the pilot study because participants are selected according to characteristics relevant to the research problem, particularly rural college-going female students who depend on bus/public transport.



\---



\## 7. Research Instruments



The research uses the following instruments:



1\. Pre-Use Survey Questionnaire

2\. Beta-Use / During-Use Feedback Questionnaire

3\. Post-Use Questionnaire

4\. Application-generated travel records

5\. Application-generated attendance-related records

6\. Open-ended student feedback



\---

\## 10. Data Collection Process



The proposed research flow is:



\*\*Problem Identification → Pre-Use Survey → Digital Intervention → Beta Testing → During-Use Feedback → Post-Use Feedback → Analysis\*\*



For longer-term evaluation:



\*\*PRE Indicators → Application Use → Observation Period → POST Indicators → PRE–POST Comparison\*\*



\---



\## 11. Quantitative Analysis



Depending on the final dataset, analysis may include:



\- Frequency

\- Percentage

\- Mean

\- Standard deviation

\- PRE–POST comparison

\- Change in attendance percentage

\- Change in late arrivals

\- Change in transport-related absences



Appropriate inferential statistical procedures should be selected according to the final research design, measurement level, sample characteristics and assumptions.



\---



\## 12. Qualitative Analysis



Open-ended responses can be organised using thematic coding.



Potential themes may include:



\- Bus Timing and Delay

\- Bus Availability

\- Waiting Time

\- Route Information

\- Bus-Stop Accessibility

\- Safety

\- Journey Planning

\- Application Usability

\- Feature Usefulness

\- Technical Problems

\- Suggested Improvements



Themes should ultimately be derived and refined from the actual participant responses.



\---



\## 13. Privacy and Confidentiality



Public research material should avoid disclosure of:



\- participant names;

\- phone numbers;

\- personal email addresses;

\- Firebase user IDs;

\- exact private/home locations;

\- identifiable consent information; and

\- other personally identifiable information.



Participant codes should be used where appropriate.



Any dataset intended for public release should undergo appropriate anonymisation and review before publication.



\---



\## 14. Interpretation Limitation



Short-duration beta testing can provide evidence regarding:



\- usability;

\- feature experience;

\- perceived usefulness;

\- satisfaction; and

\- technical issues.



It should \*\*not\*\* by itself be interpreted as evidence that the application caused improvement in attendance, reduced late arrivals or reduced transport-related absences.



Those outcomes require an appropriate observation period and analysis of collected PRE and POST data.



\---



\## 15. Current Research Status



The project currently represents a combination of:



\*\*Applied Research + Descriptive Survey + Digital Intervention + Beta Testing + Usability/Perceived-Usefulness Evaluation\*\*



A longer-term:



\*\*One-Group Pre-Test–Post-Test Evaluation\*\*



is proposed for assessing measurable travel and attendance-related outcomes.
