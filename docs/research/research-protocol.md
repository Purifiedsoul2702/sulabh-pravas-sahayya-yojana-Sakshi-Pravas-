\# Research Protocol



\## Project Title



\*\*सुलभ प्रवास सहाय्य योजना: ग्रामीण महाविद्यालयीन विद्यार्थिनींसाठी डिजिटल प्रवास सहाय्य प्रणाली\*\*



\*\*Sulabh Pravas Sahayya Yojana: A Digital Travel Assistance System for Rural College-Going Female Students\*\*



\---



\## 1. Research Problem



Rural college-going female students who depend on public bus transportation may experience difficulties such as uncertain bus schedules, delays, long waiting periods, missed buses, limited route information, distance from bus stops and safety concerns.



These difficulties may influence journey planning, punctuality and college attendance.



The project investigates these travel-related difficulties and evaluates a digital travel-assistance intervention developed to support students in managing their daily college journeys.



\---



\## 2. Research Questions



\### RQ1

What major bus and travel-related problems are experienced by rural college-going female students?



\### RQ2

What difficulties do students experience regarding bus availability, timing, waiting time, route information, bus-stop accessibility and safety?



\### RQ3

How useful do students perceive the Sulabh Pravas Sahayya Yojana application for planning and managing their college travel?



\### RQ4

Which application features are perceived as most useful by participating students?



\### RQ5

What usability and technical difficulties are experienced while using the application?



\### RQ6

What improvements and additional features do students suggest for the application?



\### RQ7

Over a suitable longer observation period, are there measurable differences between PRE and POST indicators such as attendance, late arrivals and transport-related absences?



\---



\## 3. Research Objectives



1\. To identify travel-related problems experienced by rural college-going female students.

2\. To study bus timing, availability, waiting time, route information, bus-stop accessibility and safety-related difficulties.

3\. To develop a digital intervention for supporting daily college travel.

4\. To evaluate the usability of the developed application.

5\. To assess students' perceived usefulness of different application features.

6\. To identify technical problems and areas for improvement.

7\. To analyse student feedback regarding future application development.

8\. To examine longer-term PRE–POST changes in selected attendance and travel-related indicators.



\---



\## 4. Proposed Hypotheses for Longer-Term Evaluation



The following hypotheses apply to the planned longer-term PRE–POST component and should be tested only after sufficient actual observations have been collected.



\### H01

There is no statistically significant difference between PRE and POST attendance indicators among participating students.



\### H11

There is a statistically significant difference between PRE and POST attendance indicators among participating students.



\### H02

There is no statistically significant difference between PRE and POST late-arrival indicators among participating students.



\### H12

There is a statistically significant difference between PRE and POST late-arrival indicators among participating students.



\### H03

There is no statistically significant difference between PRE and POST transport-related absence indicators among participating students.



\### H13

\## 6. Operational Definitions



\### Attendance Percentage



The proportion of recorded college days on which a participant attended college.



\*\*Attendance Percentage = (Attended Days / Recorded College Days) × 100\*\*



\### Late Arrival



A recorded college-arrival outcome in which the participant reports reaching college late.



\### Transport-Related Absence



An absence in which the participant reports that she could not attend college and identifies the absence as caused by a transport/travel-related problem.



\### Bus Delay



A participant-reported delay associated with the bus used or expected for a college journey.



\### Perceived Usefulness



The participant's assessment of whether the application or its features are useful in supporting planning, information access, travel recording, safety or management of the college journey.



\### Usability



The participant's experience of how understandable, accessible and convenient the application is to use during beta testing.

\## 8. Proposed Quantitative Analysis



Depending on the final data and assumptions, descriptive analysis may include:



\- Frequency

\- Percentage

\- Mean

\- Standard deviation



Longer-term PRE–POST analysis may include:



\- PRE and POST attendance comparison

\- PRE and POST late-arrival comparison

\- PRE and POST transport-related absence comparison

\- Change scores



The final inferential test should be selected according to the measurement level, distribution, paired nature of the observations, sample size and statistical assumptions.



\---



\## 9. Qualitative Analysis



Open-ended responses will be reviewed and coded into themes derived from actual participant responses.



Possible initial coding categories include:



\- Bus Timing and Delay

\- Bus Availability

\- Waiting Time

\- Route Information

\- Bus-Stop Accessibility

\- Safety

\- Journey Planning

\- Useful App Features

\- Technical Problems

\- Suggested Improvements

\- Additional Feature Requirements



The final themes should be based on the collected participant responses rather than being predetermined solely from these categories.

SP001

SP002

SP003

SP030

\---



\## 11. Interpretation of Current Beta Evaluation



The current short-duration beta evaluation is primarily suitable for examining:



\- usability;

\- feature experience;

\- perceived usefulness;

\- technical difficulties;

\- satisfaction; and

\- suggestions for improvement.



It is not sufficient by itself to establish that the application caused an improvement in attendance, punctuality or transport-related absence.



\---



\## 12. Planned Longer-Term Evaluation



Following an appropriate observation period, the study may compare participant-level PRE and POST indicators.



A paired design should compare the same participants where both PRE and POST observations are available.



Participants with incomplete data should be identified transparently rather than silently replaced or supplemented with fabricated observations.



\---



\## 13. Research Integrity



Actual participant responses should remain distinct from demonstration data.



If simulated data are created for:



\- software testing;

\- dashboard demonstration;

\- statistical-code testing; or

\- presentation of system functionality,



they must be clearly labelled:



\*\*SIMULATED / DEMONSTRATION DATA – NOT ACTUAL RESEARCH FINDINGS\*\*



No simulated response should be reported as an actual participant response.



\---



\## 14. Privacy



Public research outputs should exclude direct identifiers and sensitive technical identifiers.



Examples include:



\- participant names;

\- phone numbers;

\- personal email addresses;

\- Firebase UIDs;

\- authentication information;

\- exact private/home locations; and

\- identifiable consent documentation.
